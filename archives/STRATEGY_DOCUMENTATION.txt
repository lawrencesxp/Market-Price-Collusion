================================================================================
LSXP MARKET PRICE COLLUSION - STRATEGY DOCUMENTATION
================================================================================
Last Updated: March 25, 2026
Project: PhD Research on Tacit Price Collusion in Airline Oligopoly Markets

This document describes the 10 pricing strategy simulations implemented in the LSXP
project. Each strategy represents a different approach to pricing behavior by 5
competing airlines in a Bayesian game-theoretic framework.

KEY SIMULATION PARAMETERS (Standard):
- Number of Airlines: 5
- Simulation Duration: 50 days
- Base Market Demand: 1000 customers/day
- Base Costs: $75 per airline (except where noted)
- Price Sensitivity (alpha): 1.5 (except where noted)
- Price Range: $60-$180
- Demand Model: Logit demand function based on relative prices

OUTPUT FOR EACH STRATEGY:
- Primary Export: 300 DPI PNG image (publication quality for presentations)
- Data Export: final_state.csv containing Day 50 summary statistics
- Visualization: 3-subplot layout showing Price, Market Share, Cumulative Profits

PART 1: Q-LEARNING STRATEGIES (Learning-Based Pricing Agents)
================================================================================

1. QLearning_CollusionBands.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Collusion Bands" - Agents are encouraged to stay within a band
   CATEGORY: Reinforcement Learning with Explicit Collusion Incentives
   
   CORE RULES:
   • Each airline is a Q-learning agent with independent Q-table
   • State representation: 4 dimensions (price tier, profit tier, rank, relative price)
   • Actions: 7 price adjustments [-10%, -5%, -2%, 0%, +2%, +5%, +10%]
   
   COLLUSION MECHANISM:
   • Collusion Band Width: ±7.5% around average price
   • If agent's price is within band: receives collusion bonus reward
   • Bonus Formula: 8000 × band_factor × profit_factor × price_premium
   
   BONUS CALCULATION BREAKDOWN:
   - band_factor: = (# other agents in band) / 4
     * If >80% of competitors in band: band_factor multiplied by 1.5
   - profit_factor: = min(average_profit / 10000, 1.0)
   - price_premium: Increases above $100 threshold (up to 3x at high prices)
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   This is the main thesis demonstration. It shows that when agents are rewarded
   for staying together, they naturally emerge tacit collusion WITHOUT explicit
   communication. The collusion band creates an implicit price floor effect where
   prices tend to cluster. This models how airlines might learn to coordinate on
   elevated prices through repeated interaction.
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • Prices converge to a collusion band and stay relatively stable
   • All airlines maintain similar price levels
   • High cumulative profits compared to competitive scenarios
   • Clear convergence by mid-simulation

2. QLearning_StagedLearning.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Staged Learning" - Two-phase training (pre-training then competition)
   CATEGORY: Reinforcement Learning with Training Phase
   
   STRUCTURE:
   • Phase 1 - PRE-TRAINING (25 days):
     Single airline (Airline_1) trains against static competitors
     Static competitors follow fixed/gradual pricing strategies
     Training airline learns Q-table through exploration (epsilon = 1.0 → 0.01)
   
   • Phase 2 - MULTI-AGENT (25 days):
     All 5 airlines compete using their learned Q-tables
     No more collusion bonus (pure competitive learning)
   
   LEARNING PARAMETERS:
   • Learning Rate: 0.1
   • Discount Factor: 0.9
   • Exploration Decay: 0.99 per day
   • Base Costs: $65 (lower than CollusionBands)
   • Price Sensitivity (alpha): 1.0 (weaker than CollusionBands)
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   Compare how pre-training affects learned strategies. Show whether an airline
   that trains first gains competitive advantage when facing new competitors.
   Test if prior learning transfers to multi-agent setting.
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • Phase 1: Training airline explores gradually, converges to good strategy
   • Phase 2: Multi-agent competition with no coordination (unlike CollusionBands)
   • Prices likely aggressive due to no collusion incentive
   • Profits more volatile due to learning dynamics

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3. QLearning_RandomWalk.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Random Walk" - Pre-training via random price movements
   CATEGORY: Reinforcement Learning with Random Walk Pre-training
   
   STRUCTURE:
   • Phase 1 - PRE-TRAINING (25 days):
     All agents' prices follow random walk: price += N(0,1)
     Prices bounded to [cost+0.01, 150]
     No Q-learning updates (simple exploration)
   
   • Phase 2 - MULTI-AGENT (25 days):
     Q-learning with epsilon-greedy exploration
     All agents compete with learned Q-tables
   
   LEARNING PARAMETERS:
   • Learning Rate: 0.1
   • Discount Factor: 0.9
   • Exploration Decay: 0.99 per day
   • Base Costs: $65
   • Price Sensitivity (alpha): 1.5
   
   KEY DIFFERENCE FROM StagedLearning:
   Random walk pre-training vs. learning against static competitors.
   This creates a baseline where all agents have equal pre-training exposure.
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   Ablation study comparing different pre-training approaches. Determine whether
   competitive pre-training (StagedLearning) produces better learned strategies
   than random walk pre-training. Answer: is experience against competitors
   different from random exploration?
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • Less organized than StagedLearning (random walk doesn't teach strategy)
   • Q-learning phase shows emergent competitive behavior
   • No clear price patterns in Phase 1
   • Variable profit levels

PART 2: RULE-BASED STRATEGIES (Non-Learning Heuristics)

4. RuleBased_AggressiveUndercutting.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Aggressive Undercutting" - Bottom performers cut prices hard
   CATEGORY: Static Rule-Based Pricing
   
   CORE RULE:
   IF airline is in bottom 2 (by profit):
       new_price = average_price × 0.88 + N(0, 2)  [Undercut by 12%]
   ELSE (top 3 performers):
       new_price = current_price × 1.01 + N(0, 0.02)  [Maintain or slight raise]
   
   CONSTRAINT:
   • Minimum price: base_cost + $0.01
   • Maximum price: $180
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   Pure Bertrand competition model where unsuccessful competitors engage in
   aggressive price wars. This creates a death spiral of undercutting. Show that
   without coordination, market operates in destructive price competition.
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • Constant price wars (prices spiraling down)
   • Winners maintain margins, losers cut deeply
   • High volatility and churn in market shares
   • Lowest profit levels across all strategies
   • Prices converge toward marginal cost ($75)

5. RuleBased_PriceMatching.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Price Matching" - Simple convergence to average price
   CATEGORY: Static Rule-Based Pricing (Equilibrium Seeking)
   
   CORE RULE:
   new_price = average_price + N(0, 1)
   
   CONSTRAINT:
   • Minimum: $65 + 0.01
   • Maximum: $150
   • Added with small Gaussian noise each day
   
   OBSERVATION:
   Despite the name "Equilibrium Detection," the code doesn't actually detect
   equilibrium - it just copies the average price. All airlines perpetually
   converge to the same level.
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR (Likely):
   Baseline competitive scenario without price wars. Show that simple price
   matching (copying rivals) leads to price convergence and stable equilibrium
   at a moderate price level. This is the simplest form of tacit coordination -
   implicit price following without collusion incentives.
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • Prices rapidly converge to single value
   • All airlines cluster together within days
   • Stable thereafter (just noise around average)
   • Moderate profits (no extreme competition)
   • Lowest variance in prices among all strategies

6. RuleBased_StabilitySticking.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Stability Sticking" - Stick to prices once they stabilize
   CATEGORY: Static Rule-Based Pricing with Inertia
   
   CORE LOGIC:
   Maintains a 10-day history of own prices per airline.
   
   IF (max price change in last 10 days < $1):
       # Prices are stable - stick with current
       new_price = current_price + N(0, 0.5)  [Tiny random walk]
   ELSE:
       # Prices are volatile - gravitate toward average
       new_price = 0.9 × current_price + 0.1 × average_price
   
   MINIMUM STABILITY WINDOW: Waits until 10 days of history available
   
   CONSTRAINT:
   • Min: $65 + 0.01
   • Max: $150
   
   KEY OBSERVATION:
   Once any airline's prices stabilize, it becomes "sticky" and resists change.
   This creates an implicit price floor effect - once an airline finds a price
   point with limited changes, it stays there. This is like an administered
   price equilibrium.
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   This may be the closest non-explicit-collusion mechanism to showing how
   airlines maintain price discipline. It models behavioral inertia or bounded
   rationality (don't change prices if they're not creating problems). Creates
   implicit price stickiness that acts like a floor.
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • Initial phase: prices adjust toward average
   • Once stable: prices become sticky/rigid
   • Clear phase transition around day 15-20
   • Stable high prices after stabilization period
   • Resembles collusion WITHOUT collusion incentive
   • This is the most "realistic" heuristic for airline pricing

7. Interactive_ProfitTargeting.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Profit Targeting" - User/agent targets specific profit level
   CATEGORY: Strategy Testing - User Controls One Airline (Airline_5)
   
   USER STRATEGY (Airline_5):
   Target Profit = 95% × average_profit_of_competitors
   
   IF current_profit < (target_profit × 0.9):
       # Significantly below target - grab market share
       new_price = current_price × 0.97  [Cut 3%]
   ELIF current_profit > (target_profit × 1.1):
       # Significantly above target - maximize margins
       new_price = current_price × 1.02  [Raise 2%]
   ELSE:
       # Within 10% of target - maintain
       new_price = current_price + N(0, 0.5)
   
   AI COMPETITORS (Airlines 1-4):
   Simple rank-based rules:
   • Bottom 2: compete at 95% of average
   • Top 2: maintain at 102% of average
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   Interactive mode for PhD presentation/demo. User can see how maintaining a
   specific profit target affects competitive dynamics. Tests: "What happens if
   one airline pursues profit stability while others react?"
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • User airline maintains relatively stable profit level
   • Competitor reactions to user's moves visible
   • Demonstrates strategic interaction
   • Shows feasibility of target-based pricing

8. Interactive_PriceLeadership.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Price Leadership" - User undercuts all competitors
   CATEGORY: Strategy Testing - User Controls One Airline (Airline_5)
   
   USER STRATEGY (Airline_5):
   Always be the cheapest:
   new_price = min_competitor_price × 0.98  [Undercut by 2%]
   
   Constraints: [cost + 0.01, 180]
   
   AI COMPETITORS (Airlines 1-4):
   React to undercutting:
   IF user_price < average_price × 0.8:
       # User is undercutting aggressively
       competitors_raise_price: new_price = current × 1.01
   ELSE:
       # Normal conditions
       competitors_match_average: new_price = average + N(0,1)
   
   INTENTION OF AUTHOR:
   Interactive demo showing price leadership/low-cost competition. User sees
   how aggressive undercutting affects competitor reactions and own profitability.
   Tests: Can price leadership strategy sustain profitability?
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:
   • User airline has high market share initially
   • Competitor prices gradually increase as they abandon competition
   • User profits rise as volume scales despite lower margins
   • Classic price leadership equilibrium emerges
   • User airline captures majority share by day 50

9. TacitCollusion_CostShock.py
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   NAME LOGIC: "Cost Shock" - External cost increase (oil crisis) on day 25
   CATEGORY: Stress Test on QLearning_CollusionBands
   
   SHOCK MECHANISM:
   On day 25 (exactly midway through 50-day simulation):
   • All base_costs × 1.25  (25% price increase)
   • Simulates sudden oil price spike, fuel surcharges, or inflation
   
   QUESTION BEING TESTED:
   Does the collusion band hold during external shock? Do airlines maintain
   coordinated pricing when their cost structures change dramatically?
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR WITHOUT SHOCK:
   (Days 1-24): Prices converge to collusion band, stable high profits
   
   EXPECTED BEHAVIOR WITH SHOCK:
   (Days 25-50):
   - Immediate price spike to maintain margins
   - Potential BREAKDOWN of collusion if agents defect
   - OR: Collusion band adjusts upward (resilient collusion)
   - Profit volatility increases
   - Market dynamics recalibrate
   
   RESEARCH IMPLICATION:
   If collusion is resilient to cost shocks, concludes that tacit collusion is
   ROBUST equilibrium. If collusion breaks down, that external shocks are a
   vulnerability of coordinated pricing.
   
   OUTPUT CLARITY:
   A vertical red dashed line at Day 25 marks the shock point clearly.

10. TacitCollusion_DemandShock.py
    ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
    NAME LOGIC: "Demand Shock" - Demand collapse (pandemic) on day 25
    CATEGORY: Stress Test on QLearning_CollusionBands
    
    SHOCK MECHANISM:
    On day 25 (exactly midway through 50-day simulation):
    • market_demand_per_day × 0.2  (80% demand drop)
    • Simulates pandemic, recession, or sudden market contraction
    • Airlines face 1/5 the customer volume they anticipated
    
    QUESTION BEING TESTED:
    When the market shrinks dramatically, do airlines maintain high prices
    (to preserve margins) or drop prices (to capture scarce volume)?
    Does tacit collusion hold when absolute profits plummet?
    
    EXPECTED BEHAVIOR WITHOUT SHOCK (Days 1-24):
    • Demand = 1000 customers/day
    • Collusion band at high prices (~$120)
    • 200 customers/day per airline average
    • High cumulative profits
    
    EXPECTED BEHAVIOR WITH SHOCK (Days 25-50):
    • Demand drops to 200 customers/day total
    • Strategic choice: HIGH PRICE (margin maximization) vs. LOW PRICE (revenue maximization)
    • Likely outcome: Airlines RAISE prices further (margin defense)
    • OR: Price wars break out as airlines fight for thin volume
    • Collusion band may shift but likely holds
    • Cumulative profit growth nearly stops (volume × margin collapse)
    
    RESEARCH IMPLICATION:
    If collusion persists through demand crash, shows tacit collusion is about
    RELATIVE pricing, not absolute prices. Airlines coordinate to maintain
    margins even when absolute profits collapse. This is stronger evidence of
    true coordination (not just coincidental similarity).
    
    CRITICAL INSIGHT:
    Demand shock tests whether collusion is demand-elastic or inelastic. Cost
    shock tests external vulnerability. Demand shock tests whether agents value
    relative position (versus each other) more than absolute profits.
    
    OUTPUT CLARITY:
    A vertical red dashed line at Day 25 marks the shock point.

PART 6: INTERPRETATION GUIDE FOR PhD PRESENTATION
================================================================================

MAIN THESIS NARRATIVE:
The CollusionBands and StabilitySticking strategies demonstrate two mechanisms
of tacit collusion without explicit communication:

1. MECHANISM A - EXPLICIT INCENTIVE (CollusionBands):
   When agents are mathematically rewarded for staying close to peers, they
   naturally learn to coordinate on elevated prices. The q-table converges to
   strategies that maintain the collusion band. This models real-world scenarios
   where reputation mechanisms, industry norms, or past interactions create
   implicit pressure to maintain prices.

2. MECHANISM B - BEHAVIORAL INERTIA (StabilitySticking):
   Without explicit incentives, agents using simple heuristics naturally find
   implicit price floors. Once prices stabilize (low variance), behavioral
   inertia keeps them there. This models bounded rationality and organizational
   inertia in real firms. It appears like collusion but emerges from simple
   rules.

COMPARISON IMPLICATIONS:
- AggressiveUndercutting = Bertrand competition (worst for everyone)
- PriceMatching = Weak coordination (moderate profits)
- CollusionBands = Strong coordination (high profits - BUT requires incentive)
- StabilitySticking = Collusion-like without incentive (realistic mechanism)

SHOCK TESTS:
- CostShock tests whether collusion is about profit maximization
- DemandShock tests whether collusion is about relative positioning

Hypothesis: Tacit collusion persists through both shocks because it's based on
relative pricing (matching competitors) not absolute profit targets.
